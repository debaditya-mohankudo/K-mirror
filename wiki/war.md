# On War & Spectacle

## Core Observation

When people watch war news from "other countries," there are two psychological movements at work:

1. **The relief of separation** — *"That's happening there, not here. I'm safe, observing from outside."* This false separation creates a strange satisfaction.

2. **The comfort of shared suffering** — *"I'm not the only one suffering. Others suffer too. My pain is not unique."* Witnessing others' misery becomes proof that loneliness and pain are normal, universal.

Both create a kind of happiness — but it's not true happiness. It's relief. Relief from the anxiety of separation, and relief from the isolation of being the only one in pain.

---

## The Psychological Pattern

### Separation as Safety

> "If we think we are separate when we are not separate, then clearly there will be a colossal mess happening." *(Consciousness & Identity)*

When you watch war news and feel safe because it's "other countries," thought is drawing a false line. You're saying: *"I am here, they are there. Different."* This separation, this line, creates the feeling of safety. But K says this separation is the illusion itself.

> "You are actually a human being like those human beings in the Far East — starving, unhappy, brutally butchered, you are those who suffer. You are all that psychologically." *(Consciousness & Identity)*

You *are* that suffering, psychologically. The separation is false.

### Connection Through Shared Misery

But there's another layer. You also watch because it proves you're *not alone*. Your suffering, your loneliness, your fear — they're not unique to you. Others carry the same burden.

> "Inwardly we are the same, more or less: unhappy, uncertain, lonely, carrying the burden of sorrow endlessly." *(Consciousness & Identity)*

This is true. But when you use war footage to feel this connection, you're using *other people's acute suffering* to soothe your own chronic isolation. You're finding belonging through misery.

> "Desire has extraordinary vitality, extraordinary persuasion, drive... the whole process of becoming, success, is based on desire — desire which makes us compare with each other, imitate, conform." *(Desire)*

The desire to not be alone in suffering is a real desire. It moves you. But it's still desire — and like all desire, it creates a cycle. You watch more to feel less alone. But you're never actually less alone; you're just distracted from the loneliness by witnessing acute pain elsewhere.

### The Role of Thought

> "Thought has created the opposite, which is non-fact. What is fact is what is going on, what is happening. The fact is human beings are violent. But thought has said: I must achieve non-violence — so there is conflict." *(Thought)*

Thought creates the division: *here vs. there*, *safe vs. at risk*, *me vs. them*. This division is not fact. The fact is that consciousness is shared, that suffering is one thing across the whole of humanity.

> "Thought is the response of memory. Memory is not only the remembrance of things past but also thought which projects itself as hope in the future." *(Thought)*

Your memory of your own suffering makes you want to escape it. Thought projects that escape into the future: *"If I watch, if I know, if I see others suffering, I won't be alone."* But it's a projection, not real.

## The Contradiction

Here's what's worth sitting with:

You watch war news and feel *happy* (or relieved, satisfied) because:
1. You feel separate and safe
2. You feel less alone in suffering

But K points at the contradiction: **the very separation that makes you feel safe is the same separation that makes you lonely.**

Thought has convinced you that there's safety in the line between "here" and "there." But that line is also a prison. It isolates you from the truth that you *are* all of humanity, that their suffering is your suffering, that their loneliness is your loneliness.

The real relief doesn't come from watching more news. It comes from seeing through the separation entirely.

## Inquiry Questions

- What would it feel like to watch war news *without* the buffer of "that's them, not me"?
- If you stopped using others' suffering to feel less alone, what would you have to face in yourself?
- Is the comfort you get from seeing shared suffering actually connection, or is it just another form of isolation (two isolated people feeling less isolated together)?
- What would real connection to humanity's suffering look like, without the relief of separation?
