# On Image & Becoming: The Instagram Trap

## Core Observation

Instagram and social media platforms are not fundamentally about connecting with others — they're mirrors that amplify an ancient psychological mechanism: **the self's addiction to comparison and becoming**.

When you scroll, you're not just consuming content. You're perpetually measuring yourself against curated images: their beauty vs. yours, their success vs. yours, their experiences vs. yours. This comparative movement creates psychological pain. And the strange thing is, you keep doing it even though it makes you miserable — because the pain itself confirms your existence.

---

## The Psychological Pattern

### Comparison as the Essence of Suffering

Krishnamurti would say that **comparison is the very essence of suffering**. When you measure yourself against an image on the screen, you're creating a gap: what *is* (you, now) versus what *should be* (the image, the ideal).

> "Where there is desire, with its image created by thought, there must be the pursuit of pleasure. And when pleasure is thwarted, either there is fear, or antagonism, or violence. That is the root of violence." *(Desire)*

This violence is internal. You're directing aggression at yourself: *"I'm not like them. I'm not good enough. I should be different."*

> "Desire has extraordinary vitality, extraordinary persuasion, drive, achievement, and the whole process of becoming, success, is based on desire — desire which makes us compare with each other, imitate, conform." *(Desire)*

Instagram industrializes this desire. Every scroll is an invitation to want, to compare, to become.

### The Self Sustained Through Comparison

The ego, the psychological "me," exists only in relationship — through comparison, competition, measurement. Instagram is a perfect feeding mechanism for the self.

Each post triggers the same questions:
- Am I as good?
- Am I enough?
- Do I have that?
- Should I be there?

These questions are painful, but they give the self substance. *"I am the one who doesn't have that life"* — even this negative identity reinforces your sense of existing, of being.

> "Thought creates the problem psychologically and the mind is trained to solve problems — so thought, creating the problem, thought then tries to solve the problem. So it is caught in the same old process." *(Thought)*

The self creates inadequacy through comparison, then uses that inadequacy to justify endless scrolling, endless seeking. You're trapped in a loop you created.

### Images vs. Reality

Krishnamurti repeatedly pointed out that we live in images, not reality. Instagram is literally a platform of curated images — not the truth of anyone's life, but constructed representations designed to create specific impressions.

But here's the deeper issue: **you also carry an image of yourself**. When you encounter someone's Instagram image, your self-image encounters their projected image. Two unrealities are colliding, and you suffer from this collision of illusions. Neither the image they present nor the image you have of yourself is actually real.

> "There is no complete thinking. So our actions then are limited. Our observations are limited, as long as there is the functioning of thought." *(Thought)*

Your perception of both yourself and others is filtered through thought, through memory, through conclusion. You see neither yourself nor them — you see images thought has constructed.

### The Illusion of Connection While Being Profoundly Alone

People scroll endlessly through others' lives while sitting in isolation. There's a desperate seeking for connection, for relationship, but it's all mediated through images and curated moments. You're witnessing edited fragments of lives, not actual human beings.

This creates a peculiar psychological state: **you feel both connected to hundreds of people and utterly alone**. The loneliness intensifies the scrolling, which intensifies the loneliness. It's a self-perpetuating cycle.

> "We are born with fear and die with fear... fear of loneliness, fear of not being successful, fear of losing, fear of not having security." *(Fear)*

Instagram feeds on this fear. It offers the illusion of connection to soothe the fear of loneliness, but the connection is false — therefore the loneliness only deepens.

### Becoming: The Rejection of What Is

Instagram constantly shows you what you *could* become: more beautiful, more successful, more traveled, more loved. This feeds the psychological process of "becoming" — the idea that you are incomplete now and will be complete in the future.

> "There is a difference between the beauty of flowering and the striving to become something." *(Desire)*

One is natural, alive, whole. The other is violent, endless, never satisfied. Instagram keeps you in the second state perpetually.

> "So what one is now, one will be tomorrow, a thousand tomorrows. And if there is no fundamental radical psychological revolution, tomorrow will be exactly what we are now." *(Thought)*

While you're scrolling, comparing, becoming — nothing actually changes. You arrive at the future with the same mind that started this cycle. The future collapses into the present moment of suffering.

### Envy and the Pleasure of Pain

There's also a subtle pleasure in the jealousy and unhappiness. The mind seeks stimulation — even negative stimulation is better than emptiness. The pain of comparison gives you something to think about, to feel, to react to. It confirms your existence.

You might notice people can't stop scrolling even though it makes them miserable. Why? Because **the alternative — facing yourself without distraction — is more frightening than the pain of comparison**.

> "Pleasure invariably goes with fear." *(Desire)*

Your pleasure in watching others' lives is inseparable from your fear of your own emptiness.

### Everyone Is Caught in the Same Trap

Here's the radical insight: **The person you're jealous of is also suffering in exactly the same way.**

They too are comparing, seeking validation, feeling inadequate, constructing images. Their beautiful post is itself evidence of their suffering — why else would they need to present themselves this way?

> "The content of our consciousness is the common ground of all humanity... Inwardly we are the same, more or less: unhappy, uncertain, lonely, carrying the burden of sorrow endlessly." *(Consciousness & Identity)*

When you see this — not as an idea but as a living reality — the whole mechanism of comparison loses its grip. Suddenly there's compassion instead of envy. The very person making you jealous is trapped in the same psychological prison you are.

## The Real Question: Direct Observation

Krishnamurti wouldn't give you a solution or tell you to delete Instagram. Instead, he'd ask you to observe:

> "Can you watch yourself scrolling? Not judge it, not try to stop it, but actually observe: What are you seeking? When jealousy arises, can you stay with it without naming it, without trying to push it away? What happens to jealousy when you give it complete attention without the observer who is jealous?"

The point isn't to resist or transcend Instagram. It's to see the mechanism of comparison itself with such clarity that it loses its grip on you.

## Inquiry Questions

- When you scroll, what are you actually seeking? Comfort? Validation? Escape? Can you feel it?
- Is there pleasure in the jealousy? If so, what kind — stimulation, confirmation of your inadequacy, proof that you exist?
- What are you afraid would happen if you stopped scrolling and sat with yourself?
- Can you observe the jealousy completely — without judging it, without trying to change it — and see what it actually is?
- Who is the person you're comparing yourself to? Is that person real, or is it an image thought has constructed?
- If everyone posting on Instagram is caught in the same trap of seeking validation through images, what does that tell you about the trap itself?
- Why do you need to be anything other than what you are right now?
- What would it feel like to look at Instagram — or anything — without the comparative mind? Is that even possible for you?
