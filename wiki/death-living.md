# On Death & Living

> "Death means the ending of that memory... I am frightened to let all that go, which means death."

> "Living, to all the things of memory, ending that — which is death. That means death is not separate from living."

> "Can we end voluntarily, easily, without any effort, that form of attachment? Which means dying to something we have known."

> "Death is not something far away, death is not something that is at the end of one's life — but rather living, to all the things of memory, ending that."

> "That which has been put together by thought — when thought ends, the thing that has been put together naturally dissolves. So before I die, can I, living, dissolve this thing?"

> "That consciousness with its content can be ended while I am living. Which means, while I am living I am dying."

> "One doesn't wait for death to come at the end of time, but to live with death, which is the ending. Then there is tremendous energy — compassion — and with it comes intelligence."

> "Is there ever an ending to anything in our life? Or is there a constant continuity of the same thing in different directions? I give up this pattern and take on another pattern."

> "Will you end something which gives you great pleasure, without any motive, without any action of will? End it. You can't argue with death, which is the ending."

> "We are all going to die one day; that is a fact. And we have never gone into the question because most people are afraid of it — what is death, what is it to die?"

> "Why have we made death something far away from life, living? We are living now, and death may come when we are ninety, a hundred or later — but knowing that the now contains the future, therefore death is the now."

> "We are frightened of living. What we call living is a fearful turmoil, conflict, struggle, pain, anxiety — the everlasting, constant waking up in the morning and rushing off to the office or to the factory."
