# On Desire

> "The beginning of desire begins with sensation, sensory responses — then thought creates the image, at that moment begins desire."

> "If there was no thought there would be only sensation — not all the problems created by desire."

> "Desire has extraordinary vitality, extraordinary persuasion, drive, achievement, and the whole process of becoming, success, is based on desire — desire which makes us compare with each other, imitate, conform."

> "Not to suppress desire, not to run away from it, not to transcend it, but to understand it, to look at it, to see the whole momentum of it."

> "Seeing, touching, sensation — then thought creates the image of living in that house, being with that woman or man, driving that car — then desire begins. Where there is sensation and the operation of the senses, thought creates the image, and at that moment desire is born."

> "Will is the essence of desire. Most of our actions are directed by will: 'I will do this, I will become that, I will not do this, I should do that.' This action of will is part of our violence."

> "Where there is desire, with its image created by thought, there must be the pursuit of pleasure. And when pleasure is thwarted, either there is fear, or antagonism, or violence. That is the root of violence."

> "Illusion exists as long as there is the desire, pleasure, fear and sorrow."

> "There is a difference between the beauty of flowering and the striving to become something."

> "Enjoyment is something entirely different from pleasure. The perception of that sense of enjoyment becomes a memory, then that memory becomes the pleasure — because it has created an image of that excellent morning, and the pursuit of that image as a remembrance. Remembered beauty is not beauty."

> "Pleasure invariably goes with fear."

> "Desire is not love. And pleasure — basically, deeply, the pursuit of pleasure — that pleasure is not love."

> "Our action is based on reward and punishment. All our action has a motive, a direction, selfish generally, self-interest, self-concern, which is reward and punishment."

> "There is never action per se — action for itself, like a good carpenter who will make a marvellous cabinet, the love of it itself, not the reward, the punishment, the gain."

> "Is love the action in itself? Not the love that has jealousy, hate, amusement, fun and excitement, sex, pleasure. When there is love there is action without conflict."

> "Love is not a slave to time. If you can understand that, explain and deeply grasp the truth of it, then the brain becomes extraordinarily vital, strong, not confused in any way."
